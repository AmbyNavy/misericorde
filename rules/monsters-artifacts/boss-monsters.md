# Boss Monsters

What can be considered a “boss fight” need not depend on a specific enemy species, but can just as well depend on the game story structure. For example, fighting a dragon as a result of rolling poorly on a random encounter table may be a “regular” fight, while the goblin shaman at the climax of the adventure may be considered a boss fight—despite the obvious fact that the dragon’s default stats make her a much more terrible foe than any goblin. 

- Bosses emit a certain aura of danger that triggers an extra reserve in player characters. They each get a recharge when going into a boss battle, either in the form of a previously “used up” slot or class ability, or in the form of a 2d6 HP recharge (which can’t take HP above the character’s current maximum). 
- Any character whose HP would be reduced to −2 or less by a boss attack must save to stay at **1 HP** or take the damage and start dying. Unless healed, *any* further damage during the boss fight, even if it is a single point, means instant death, without save. 
- Bosses get an extra move, special action or extra attack at full AV each round. They may put it **in any place in the initiative order, except first**. After this extra action they may designate who takes her action for the round, which may be an ally or an enemy. After that character or monster has acted, initiative returns to its regular order. 
- If it can be explained in the game fiction, bosses may step a damage die from d6 to d10 once more per round than would otherwise be allowed. Crit damage rolls of 1 *or* 2 yield crit table rolls (p. 58).

## Boss Stages

In addition to the above special rules, bosses may have *stages*, which are conditioned and designed by the Referee. 

A stage is a version of the boss with its own HD, DF, MV, keywords, appearance and XP value (normally *not* with its own LT notation, though). The most common condition for a change of stage is that the HP of the previous stage have been reduced to zero. For example, a boss necromancer may have a second shadow stage that only appears after its human stage has been vanquished. A boss is not truly dead until all its stages have been defeated.

## Partitioning

Bosses or boss stages may divide themselves into lesser parts, no more than one per attack, each getting a cut of the total (remaining) hp. The SV, DF and AV values may deviate from the ones of the unpartioned boss or stage, but the deviation sums for each stat must be zero (e.g. for one part to have SV +2, another part must have SV −2). A part AV cannot be less than 10, and DF no less than zero. Barring special abilities and weird bodies, the parts have the same MV and make corresponding moves on the same initiative, but act as individuals in all other respects. 

When a part is killed, the Referee evaluates the situation—in particular if partitioning follows a monster’s anatomy: a severed head means instant death, but a severed tail may simply mean poor balance. Some bosses can re-partition mid-battle, as a full round action requiring concentration (p. 59).
