# Common Special Cases
To **pick a lock**, the character needs lockpicks. Make a trained roll for agility. Locks vary widely in quality—a modification is commonly assigned (as in “a +2 padlock”). A new, quality lock is −2. Exception- ally hard locks, such as a vault lock system, may be more exciting to run as auctions. Use 15 as an average vault system’s “attribute.”

**Sneaking or hiding** is a *granular* contest agility vs. willpower if someone is actively on guard (as willpower includes focus and awareness). Otherwise just use an agility roll. In a group, everyone rolls, but if someone with a relevant group helps the rest, they get +2 to their attributes. Ties are re-rolled.

**Searching** has no set attribute. It depends on what, how and where you seek. If you are precise enough, no roll is needed. De- tecting a trap, hidden door, someone sneaking or a distant sound depends on awareness and focus, i.e. willpower. Searching a library on the other hand is an intelligence roll. Sometimes, a search roll can give you a much-needed clue.

**Traps** can be handled like locks—that is roll agility to disarm one. The difference is that fumbling sets off the trap, and you get no save to avoid the consequences. Some traps can be handled without a roll, just by telling the Referee exactly what the character does to disarm them. However, the player can’t design her own challenge by telling the Referee how a trap *should* work, or give a medieval thief extensive, modern technical knowledge. Usually, you don’t want this to draw out in time and take over the session.

**Bashing a chest or door** normally requires no roll, but you do need some kind of tool that can wear down the chest or door material. It makes a lot of noise and takes time (10 minutes). Fragile items, such as vials, flasks or brittle figurines, invariably break when you bash the container that they are in.

**To push a stuck door**, first roll a d6 to see how many rounds (next page) the attempt takes. When the attempt is completed, roll for strength to see if you succeed. A crit means that you can act normally, doing something else, in the final round of the attempt. An attempt can be aborted in any round after the first (you have to test a door to see how hard it is stuck). If you fail an attempt, you can try *once* more (this takes 10 minutes). If you alter your chances significantly, such as getting someone to help out, you can always try again as if it was your first attempt.
