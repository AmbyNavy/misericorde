# Funds & Equipment

*Whitehack* has a crown (cr) currency for funds. The crown is usually a coin, made of a metal designated by the setting or module, but it could be something more esoteric in a non-fantasy game. You can use “credit” interchangeably with the same cr abbreviation. A setting can have a more intricate but flavorful system for funds. Use that instead, but establish a connection between the standard there and cr, to keep a point of reference. 

Now buy some gear. Roll a d20 under or equal to sv to get your best attribute × 10 as starting crowns, otherwise you get 3d6 × 10. Equipment lists can’t be comprehensive—add below as you see fit. To find a suitable price, try to find something already listed that you think is of equivalent value. 

Weapons, shields and armor are listed with data and prices in the combat rules (p. 61).

# Encumbrance

The character is allowed ten regular items, plus five in a backpack, sack or similar container. Heavy items count as two, while minor objects count as half. Coins, gems and very small things are nosize items—100 of them count as one regular item. Use common sense to settle what is what. Weapons have regular/heavy/minor designators. Armors, unless magick, count as per DF. 

For every completed uneven number of regular items exceeding the character’s limit, she moves down a category in the Movement Rates table (p. 53). High strength can often alleviate the consequences.

# Defense Value

Note the DF value of the character’s armor (p. 61) with any bonuses (like from a shield or a magick item) or penalties factored in. For example, a character wearing chainmail and a shield has DF 5. Unarmored characters have DF 0. You may be used to other, legacy armor systems in this tradition. The Attacking section (p. 56) deals with them. 

For magick armor, DF doesn’t have to correspond to weight and bulk. The Referee decides how the magick affects encumbrance.

\* Shevvak is a combination of shovel and short-sword. Snow Monkeys can be trained as pets, while Zoruks are fur lizards used as riding animals. All three are common in winter settings, but they are also examples of how to adapt the generic equipment list to match a particular world. Swap them for a more generic “broadblade,” dog and horse if you like, or, in a modern setting, you could swap them for a machete, simple bot and a motorcycle.
