# Combat

First determine the reaction of the enemies the party has encountered. If either side wants to fight, the combatants act and results take effect in order of initiative. When everyone has acted, a new round begins with the same order. This continues until the battle is over, through the order may be changed as part of combat.

## Attitudes & Parley

The Referee freely determines the attitudes of any non-player character—sometimes as a part of preparations, at other times as an obvious consequence of the events preceding an encounter. When it isn’t evident what an attitude might be, she can randomize it:

The attitudes may be swayed with a charisma roll if someone parleys as per above. Each additional step of change is −2, not knowing a mutual language is −6. The Referee can modify the reaction roll, and also make some monsters harder or easier to affect.

## Initiative & Surprise

At the beginning of combat, everyone rolls initiative with a d6 modified by high agility (p. 30)—high roll wins. The Referee can choose to roll once for groups of creatures, who then act on the same turn. If two combatants have tied initiatives, missiles go before melee and lighter weapons go before those that are heavier or need to be drawn. This is usually only relevant if at least one of the tied combatants is attacking the other.

 - Those who *only* wait during a round get to change their initiative to 6 in the next and all consecutive rounds. 
 - Anyone can choose a lower initiative instead of the one rolled. In the first round after this choice, the combattant can let her actions happen just before others’ on that initiative (as in “I’ll throw when the anchorite comes through the snow!”). 
 - The Referee may rule that someone is surprised when the battle begins, for example due to a failed detection roll. The surprised can only move and ready weapons, last in the first round. They don’t roll initiative until the second round. Others have combat advantage against them.

## Attacking

To attack, you add or subtract any bonuses or penalties to the character’s av and make a task roll. In order to hit, the roll must be successful *and* have a quality above the opponent’s defense. For example, someone with AV 14 and a +1 sword, fighting a DF 3 goblin, will hit on 4–15. Modifiers for equipment can be factored in on the character sheet, making the operation even simpler. 

All rules for task rolls apply, including fumbles, crits and the opportunity to use surplus points to raise quality (i.e. for a modified AV above 20). Fumbles and crits also have additional rules (p. 58). Deft characters sometimes use two dice in attacks: a successful pair means a hit and combat advantage in the next round.

## Actions During a Round

In a combat round, you may attack, make an average move (sometimes modified) and take a small action like dropping or readying a weapon. 

These actions are interchangeable from left to right, but not the other way. You can, for example, sacrifice your attack to make two moves and a small action, but you can’t sacrifice a small action to get an extra attack. Some actions and abilities require a full round, at the Referee’s discretion.

## Melee & Reach

Two enemies within five feet of each other are considered to be **engaged** in melee combat if at least one of them is attacking with a close combat weapon, or with a natural close combat weapon such as a fist or claw. Some weapons have **reach**, which extends this distance to ten feet.

## Free Attacks & Disengaging

Fighting requires focus. Casting a spell, quaffing a potion, taking something out of a backpack and similar actions give every ready and engaged enemy an *opening* for a free regular attack, regardless of turn. The Strong can make two free attacks per round (each requiring a separate opening), others one. 

To disengage without opening for free attacks, face your opponent and *carefully (p. 54) move backwards out of her reach.*

## Missile Attack Specifics

You need to be within range to attack with missile weapons, such as a pistol, bow or throwing axe. The range values on p. 62 are the close ranges. The attack gets −1 AV for each exceeded increment, up to a maximum of −4 (longer shots are impossible). 

When firing into a melee, you must beat the target’s DF by 4+ in order to hit it. If you beat it by less, you hit someone else in the melee, at the Referee’s discretion. A miss is still a miss, though. 

Some missile attacks target areas. Hitting a certain spot is a task roll. Hitting a moving target’s spot is a missile attack against DF 0. Anyone in the target area gets to save to avoid damage. The area of effect is normally 15×15 feet, or 9 grid squares. The squares can be arranged into a cone for breath weapons, or spread differently in a tight space. For ray weapons, count the number of squares crossed in the line of fire (9 squares = 45 ft).

## Crits & Fumbles

Crits do double damage, stacking with deft double damage. **A one on the damage die** (1–2 in boss fights, p. 122) also requires a roll on the crit table. Fumbles require a roll on the fumble table instead, but player characters can save to avoid consequences. For both crits and fumbles, magick weapons, armor and shields are excepted from any table-based breakage or wear.

## Combat Advantage

Some situations and positions will yield bonuses for a combattant in the form of +2 AV and damage. The Referee decides when it occurs, but flanking, higher ground, sure footing vs. loose footing, striking from behind[^1] or attacking a surprised enemy are typical cases. Sometimes, the Referee may even grant a double or triple combat advantage in the form of a +4 or +6 bonus to both av and damage.

## Conditions & Concentration

Magick, abilities or situational circumstances can daze, stun, blind, hold, deafen, confuse etc. a character or monster. Positive effects can be cover, invisibility, blurring, increased speed etc. The Referee interprets unregulated cases using combat advantage, free attacks and penalties or bonuses to df, damage, move, initiative or av. 

If a miracle worker takes damage while producing magick, she must save or have her concentration broken, which ruins the miracle completely.

## Special Combat Options

A few formalized combat options can be used by any character. They are advantageous in specific circumstances, not generally “better.” Use them in sequence with class abilities to get patterns suiting various group tactics, fighting styles and character concepts.

- **Charge** a target 15 feet away, in a straight line, to attack with combat advantage. You get −2 DF and −1 SV vs. special attacks until your next turn.
- **Defend** by taking either −2 AV, −2 damage output, or −1 AV and −1 damage output, to get +2 DF and +1 SV vs. special attacks. 
- **Fight offensively** with +1 AV and +1 damage output by taking −2 DF and −1 SV vs. special attacks, until your next turn. 
- **Grapple** a *reasonably sized* opponent by making a regular unarmed attack, then forsaking damage. The opponent must win a contest against your strength to break free. 
- **Trick** an enemy by attacking and exchanging your damage roll for one of the following options, each negated by an enemy save: **Push** the enemy five feet backwards or diagonally backwards, and follow into her former space if you like. **Feint** to make two attacks with combat advantage in the next round. **Disarm** the enemy so that her weapon drops 2d6 feet away. **Trip** the enemy so that she loses one move in the next round, giving you combat advantage. 
- **Protect** by making a *task roll* against your AV (i.e. not an attack). If you succeed, the next attack on an adjacent friend targets you instead. Enemies may save to avoid it. 
- **Press** by taking a penalty to AV for a special combat option. Give your opponent the same penalty to her save. If you fail or if the save succeeds, the enemy gets combat advantage against you in the next round. (This option only makes mathematical sense for a (modified) AV of 20+, or against very high SV.)
