# Damage, HP & Death

When a character is hit, you roll damage and subtract it from her current harm points. If hp is still positive, nothing happens (but the character is closer to getting into real trouble). If hp is zero or negative, use the below table:

### Zero & Negative HP

| HP          | Consequence                              |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 0           | Knocked out until healed to positive HP. |
| -1          | As above. Also injured.                  |
| -9 to -2    | As above. Also save or die in d6 rounds. |
| -10 or less | Instant death.                           |
{: .compact-table }

A successful first aid attempt stops bleeding as well as dying, but if it is interrupted (it takes 10 minutes to complete), the dying process starts again. Killing a defenseless target who has negative hp is a *task* requiring unbroken concentration. The Referee rules on how long it takes and whether some roll is required.

## Save Against Incoming Damage

Player characters also have the option to save against incoming damage, **once per battle**. This is an important lifeline, which can make a character playable even if she starts with a single HP:

- A successful save reduces the damage by d6 points, representing stubborn grit or an adrenaline rush that enables the character to shrug off some damage from a single attack.
- If the save fails, however, the character takes full damage and is dazed from her attempt to ignore it. Until after her next turn, she can’t attack, may only move five feet, and enemies have combat advantage against her.
- Saving against incoming damage doesn’t heal any previous damage, and can not be used to reduce harm costs for miracles.

When you would save against damage or death, you can make a **toughness task roll instead**, to the same effects. This can be better at lower levels, but at higher levels, the chance to save is greater for all but the hardiest of characters. A toughness roll and a regular save can often be described differently.
