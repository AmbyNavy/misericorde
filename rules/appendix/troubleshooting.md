# Troubleshooting

Any game can run into trouble, and *Whitehack* is no exception. Here are some topics that may be of use.

## The characters seem weak or die too often 

The most important sections on this topic are on pp. 62 ff. If you are playing a module for another system, remember that you can add a module specific AV bonus (p. 68). In some modules, characters and monsters also have more HP. Make sure you aren’t using those values directly—you should plug the **HD** values into *Whitehack*. 

If the problem persists, it can be a matter of play style. Don’t change your style! Try starting characters at level 2 or 3 if they tend to die early. Alternatively, raise HP for all characters by adding their respective class hds for level 1 to each level HD. For example, a level 3 Wise Druid gets HD 3+2, instead of HD 2+1. 

## It is hard to agree on things 

First use the table of contents and the index to make sure you aren’t trying to agree on things that are actually hard coded. Then try different ways of agreeing. You can take turns deciding, do rockpaper-scissors, vote, roll dice, etc. If the problem persists, players can have an “override marker” each. If someone dislikes a ruling, she can override it using a marker, but the Referee may use that marker later on, against any player, to force a re-roll of any kind. This will soon result in a group discussion about what is reasonable. Listen carefully to one another! 

## Miracles are too open-ended 

Miracle costs are to be *triangulated* by way of vocation, miracle wording and a desired effect. As a group, you may be playing with a bit too low costs. Also, be strict about chosing miracle wordings *permanently*, differentiating between active and inactive miracles, and having every use and cost serves as a **precedent for future uses**. If the problem persists, codify costs by making a more extensive version of the miracle HP guidelines (p. 37), adapted for *your* setting. Once things flow well, you can drop those guidelines.

## Fights are static or take too long 

Fights can become static if the Referee doesn’t play her characters to their limit using the system. They should be using free attacks, charging, pushing, tricks and combat advantage to survive. Fights should be fought in places with interesting features—both advantageous positions and danger zones. Bosses are generally bosses of someone: for more dynamic boss fights, deploy minions. 

If fights take too long despite third party HD being plugged into *Whitehack* instead of direct hp values, try adding a simple escalation mechanic: for every round after the third, **
** damage done gets an incremental +1 bonus. 

## The characters are too rich or poor 

Modules from different eras vary vastly in the amounts of available treasure, which affects levelling. If the characters level too fast or too slow, the rate can be adjusted with quest XP. When funds are used for XP, make sure the amounts are actually paid (p. 47). 

## The open spaces require too much of the Referee 

Remember that the Referee isn’t supposed to use all the open spaces herself, and certainly not as part of her preparations. The players should do a lot of the “filling” as they play, while the Referee rules on it. The rulings should then rest in the gaming group’s **non-written** collective memory. It is all right if things aren’t perfect or if the Referee can’t answer all questions. Give each other suggestions and get into the habit of dealing with things together!
