Strong characters rely on combat skills and physique. They can for example be warriors, guards, brigands, knights, bounty hunters or barbarians. They get the same, single basic attack per round as the other classes, but two free attacks (p. 57—others get one). Also, whenever they put an enemy at zero or negative harm points, they may use the flow of that action to attack another enemy, adjacent to the Strong (melee) or to the prior target (ranged). This takes a separate attack roll and a ready weapon. The number of flow attacks per round is limited to the character’s raises + 1.

Strong characters invest themselves in conflicts and may “loot” them afterwards, using one of the below categories:

- **Special conflict.** The player notes how the conflict was special. It should stand out either as a new experience or as culturally important (e.g. some faith may have sacred conflicts). The Strong character may later take +2 for one round to either DF, AV, SV, HP, an attribute, damage, healing, MV, quality or initiative—if it relates to the memory of the conflict. For example, remembering and imitating a banshee-like war cry could yield +2 to initiative.
- **Substance**. If an enemy has a suitable keyword, the Strong can extract a rare and potent substance (poison, acid, something flammable etc.) from its corpse. Some creatures may yield substances to any character examining their remains. Such findings aren’t keyword based and don’t fall under these rules.
- **Supernatural.** If an enemy has a supernatural non-violent ability, like a wyrm’s *sense gold*, and the Strong delivers the killing blow, the ability can be transferred to the Strong (it should work the same).

Conflict loot must be one of these kinds, and The Strong can only hold **one** loot instance at any time, although it can be exchanged after new conflicts (unused substances are then considered spoiled). Each instance is usable a number of times equal to the character’s level. It does not occupy a slot, but a substance may need a place in the character’s inventory, where it counts toward her encumbrance. 

The Strong can use any special combat option (p. 60), but as they advance, the Strong also fill their slots permanently with options from their ability list. Unless otherwise noted, effects last **one round** and can be activated **any number of times**.

1. As a full round action, the Strong may protect an adjacent character by letting all attacks directed at her until their next turn target them instead. Each enemy is allowed a save against this effect.
2. After a successful attack, the Strong may eschew damage to force an opponent to a space designated by the Strong, within ten feet from the opponent’s prior location. In melee, as a free move, the Strong may take that prior place instead. The target of this effect is allowed a save to negate it. The target may become subject to free attacks as a consequence of the maneuver.
3. When fighting big opponents, the Strong can use one action to cling onto and even climb them with a successful agility roll, gaining double combat advantage (+4 av and damage, see p. 59) in the next and any subsequent round as long as they hang on. New agility rolls may be required at the Referee’s discretion, but don’t require separate actions. Anything that is clearly bigger than the character’s species—like a human to a halfling—counts as a big opponent.
4. In a successful attack, the Strong may eschew a damage roll and cause −2 initiative, −10 MV, or two points of ongoing damage per round. The player must describe how this is achieved. A save can halt the last two conditions after at least one round in effect.
5. The Strong can give an ally double combat advantage once per battle, for a single attack made right away or later in that fight. This requires a *tactical* small verbal action, like “strike when she flies!”
6. With a small verbal action, the Strong may encourage their friends in a 15 feet radius, giving them +1 AV and +1 SV. This ability can be inverted to put fear in enemies (15 ft radius, −1 AV and SV).
7. The Strong can forsake their move and make a melee and a ranged attack in one round, if they have suitable, one-handed weapons (e.g. a broadsword and a throwing knife).
8. The Strong can parry and wait instead of attacking. They get +2 DF in the parrying round, then double combat advantage in the next round against a parried enemy. They can parry two consecutive rounds to get *triple* combat advantage. If damaged in a parrying round, they must save or lose the effect.

The Strong get +1 AV for a strength of 13 or higher and +1 damage too for 16 or higher. They get +1 to HP at 13 toughness and another +1 at 16. They may use all armors and weapons, and get SV bonuses vs. poison and death (+1) as well as special melee attacks (+4).
