# Beyond Levels

What happens beyond level 10? Does the game have to end? Of course not! First of all, taking a character from level 1 to level 10 is no small feat, so you needn’t worry about it for a good while unless you specifically want to start a high level campaign. But for characters who do reach the highest level and fight on, there are a few extra rules that you may use.

For each 300,000 additional xp beyond level 10, regardless of which class the character belongs to, you can:

- Move groups among the character’s attributes. 
- Exchange one class ability or miracle for another. 
- Move a previous attribute raise to another attribute. 
- Re-roll the character’s harm dice to get better hp. 
- Change the color of the character’s save.

The character’s level remains at 10, though. If you are playing with aspects (below), you may also exchange an aspect, drop an aspect, add a new aspect or bring an additional piece of material each session for an aspect that you already have.

## Divine Aid & Debts

At level ten, if not before, the gods take interest in the characters. A character may “lock” an affiliation group to a god or power, in exchange for divine aid. The god will require service in return, and until that debt is paid, the group remains unusable. Failure to obey a god when you are indebted results in more groups or even ability slots being locked in the same manner, at the Referee’s discretion. 

This mechanism means that the character is able to perform more powerful feats, but at a cost that constantly creates more trouble and adventure for her. The Referee can allow bargaining for the services and debts. This bargaining may include writing blank checks—“the god will require you to act on its behalf later on, in a way disclosed later.” Remember that gods rarely play nice.

## Playing with Aspects

Starting at level 10, players may control limited aspects of the game world, such as Inns, Bureaucracy, Books, Weather, Guards, Passageways, Spies, Politics, News—or some subset of either! The Referee decides what is available and what isn’t.

Aspects require some significant character attachment to it, like a position in politics, owning a lot of inns or being in charge of a major library. Before each game session, the player is responsible for preparing something—an interesting inn, a new law, a piece of news etc.—which she can introduce in a manner relating to her character any time she wishes. But the Referee handles it from there, the way she sees fit. For example, the player might say that a character’s spies bring news of a new mountain pass being discovered, which could facilitate the party’s travels significantly. The Referee has to accept what the player says as a fact, but she is still the Referee if the party tries to go through that pass.

Aspects let players into Referee territory and keep the group alert! They can easily be combined with the rules for randomized faction reactions, and they work well with bases (p. 88), modus rolls (p. 113) and macros (p. 114).
