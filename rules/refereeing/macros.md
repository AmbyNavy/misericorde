# Macros

For mind map nodes that you expect to come into play very often in ways that are hard to predict, or for campaign features that are very complex and therefor hard to easily hold in your head (such as large historical movements and changes that permeate the entire game world), you can create *macros*—character stat-blocks with non-character names. 

Take a look again at the example characters (p. 28). If any of them were yours, you would let them react to in-game events, you would make suitable choices at new levels, and you would have no trouble making equipment changes. You know how to play them, often to the point of making split second decisions. With that in mind, swap out the names. Imagine Howl as “The Plague,” Ogil as “The Printing Revolution” or Myrvyn as “The Secret Police.” Macros are about using character stat blocks to embody larger concepts. They make it easier to intuitively and without preparation come up with responses to events in the game fiction, and also to develop those concepts through regular leveling. 

For example, if The Plague was confined by a city lockdown, you might have it try to sneak out into the countryside with a roll for agility. If The Secret Police wanted to infiltrate the City Council, you could have it seduce a member by way of the attuned makeup box (see Myrvyn’s equipment). When leveling The Printing Revolution, you would pick powerful affiliation groups, for example in education and politics, to call upon when threatened. 

Playing a macro is just like playing a character, only the macro acts in a different stratum of the game fiction. It stays concentrated to a simple, concise stat-block, but through its actions and their consequences, it can *expand* to the layer where the characters act and experience game world events.

## Designing a Macro

To design a macro, simply follow the bullet list for character generation (p. 28), with the one difference that you are generating a macro entity instead of a character. Name it, pick a class, roll attributes— all of it. You may want to use the optional rules for customized attributes (p. 77) and rare classes (p. 80) to make the most of it, and remember that you can also level the macro (p. 31). You will find that your choices during the generation process makes you think a lot about how you see the large-scale phenomenon or faction that takes form. This in itself can be a valuable exercise.

## Invisible & Visible Macros

The players need not know about the occasional macro any more than they need to know about the Referee’s mind map or list of events. But if you have a lot of very large scale macros that don’t directly target the party in their actions, you can add a **macro phase** to your list of adventure phases, and distribute the macros for the players to control. Simply look back at the latest developments in the game world and ask the players if their macros react. Roll dice when needed, and interpret the results together. Note that macros might very well cooperate or conflict with other macros, and they may tie into aspects (p. 79). In the latter case, give such a macro to a different player than the aspect owner.

## Interpreting Macro Stat-Blocks

Classes and groups work the same as with regular characters, and some class abilities, in particular those of the Wise and the Deft, are easy to use. Other abilities require more interpretation:

- As a general guideline, pick abilities that you can easily imagine a macro use for. 
- For harder cases, look at the topical definitions of the ability. For example, the Strong abilities are to protect, to push, to fight a larger enemy, to weaken, to be tactical, to radiate an aura, to split an attack and to strike with thought and precision. You can apply a hefty bonus, like +6, to a macro task roll where an ability applies, or let the macro automatically do something relating to the ability. 
- A macro’s level tells you how weathered it is (SV), its ability to strike with force (AV) and how much it would take to destroy it (HP). This doesn’t mean that you regularly use these values, or necessarily use them at all. But they contribute to the whole and let you compare with other macros.

When playing a macro as a character, rolls are only occasional. Don’t concern yourself with every detail of magick or combat. Stick to simple rolls based on the attributes, sometimes with a comparison or auction.
