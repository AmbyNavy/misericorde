# Healing

In addition to the various magick means of restoring harm points, a character who has **not gone below zero HP** will restore all harm points completely with 48 hours of rest. It is not a healing rate, but a static side effect of prolonged rest, and isn’t affected by wise expedited healing rules. 

The following rules apply when the character does not have time to rest:

- The character recovers 1 HP every evening and morning, 2 HP if she succeeds at a toughness task roll.
- The Referee can allow a character to bind 1d6−3 HP worth of wounds immediately following a battle, with a **trained** task roll. Fumbling means that the patient loses 1 HP instead. The character can only recover harm points lost during that battle.

## Injuries & Disadvantages

If, on the other hand, the character has reached negative HP, she is **injured**. She will heal at her usual rate, but **only to HP 1**. Regular healing potions won’t increase HP further either (unless preceded by a major miracle to remove injury). Additionally, **the condition is equivalent of carrying five additional regular items**.

- To remedy this condition and recover more harm points, the character must be treated by someone with a healer vocation—a physician, a healer, an old crone, etc.
- When a healer is hard to find or the character needs to get back on her feet fast in order to survive, the player can remove the injured condition by giving the character a **permanent disadvantage**, noted next to an attribute on the character sheet. It counts in regard to how many groups the attribute has, and thus effects how future groups can be placed or have to be moved. The disadvantage can be something like two lost fingers, a limp or a disfiguring scar, and the Referee can use it to demand negative double rolls or to nullify the positive double roll benefit of a group for a particular task.
- When someone uses a weapon’s **knock-out** special ability, the resulting damage can’t kill but only knock out (this is sometimes called “subdual” or “non-injuring” damage). This means that you heal normally even from negative HP.

## Magick Interference & Healing

Healing causes *magick interference* if used to replenish what it expends, like when using a Scroll of Healing on yourself. Save for nothing to happen. Failure yields a chaotic, bad effect at the Referee’s discretion (death, petrification, a huge corruption spike etc.).

Habitual miracle workers paying hp like the Wise get constant, “benign” interference as a side effect of their trade. They regain hp at twice the natural rate of others, but it blocks any additional means of direct restoration. This includes healing potions, healing miracles and even conventional medicine. They still require medical treatment (including concoctions, herbs and first aid) to stop bleeding, to have their innate healing continue beyond 1 hp, to cure illnesses, to draw poison, or to remedy various conditions.
