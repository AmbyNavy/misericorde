# Time & Movement

Minutes, hours, months or even years pass in the game world at the Referee’s discretion. Additionally, a **round** (r) is about 10 seconds, and a **turn** is a character’s place to act in that round[^1]. When the rules say “until her next turn,” it means until the character acts in the next round.

- **1 r:** Combat round actions (p. 56). Quaff a potion. Begin first aid (stops dying). Perform a quick miracle. Kick in a door.
- **d6 r:** Listen at a door. Get something from a backpack. Pick a normal lock.
- **10 m:** Fight–loot–rest. Pick a hard lock. Search a room. Examine a potion. Finish first aid. Bash a chest. Search a door, item or spot for traps.
- **60 m:** Burn out a regular torch.
- **d6 h:** Search a library. Gather rumors. Figure out an artifact. Advanced medical aid.
- **6h:** Minimum full night’s sleep. Switch abilities or miracles into slots. Travel “a day’s journey” on foot or on a zoruk.

## Travel Times

For most terrain types, vehicles don’t affect travel times, but make the journey safer and more comfortable. Naturally, waterways require ships.

### Typical Miles per Day Travel Rates

| Terrain Type     | Max Rate | Bad Weather | Night Travel |
| ---------------- | :------: | :---------: | :----------: |
| Plains           | 24       | 12          | 18           |
| Mountains        | 12       | 6           | 6            |
| Forest, Desert   | 18       | 12          | 12           |
| Arctic           | 14       | 0           | 10           |
| Sea, Lake, River | 48       | 24          | 36           |
| Under ground     | 6        | 6           | 6            |

## Indoor Movement 

Humanoids of human and somewhat larger size (like ogres) use the Human column below, while halflings, goblins and other smaller humanoids use the Smaller column. The rates can be modified **−10/−2 if you move carefully**, and **×2 if you run**. The Referee can demand a strength roll to move at all with very heavy burdens. 

The “Crawling Rates” are used when the party moves through a dangerous environment, such as a dungeon, while mapping the environment and checking for traps and hidden passages.

### Movement Rates

| Total Burden | MV (ft/sq) for Human | MV (ft/sq) for Smaller | Crawl (ft/10m) |
| ------------ | :------------------: | :--------------------: | :------------: |
| Normal       | 30/6                 | 25/5                   | 120            |
| Heavy        | 25/5                 | 20/4                   | 100            |
| Severe       | 20/4                 | 15/3                   | 80             |
| Massive      | 15/3                 | 10/2                   | 60             |

## Straining and Boosted Movement

Any character with a heavy or worse load can choose to **strain** herself to move as per the burden category above the one she is in. Make a standard strength roll. If it is successful, the character gets the current **time unit**—round, 10 min, hour or 6 hours—of boosted movement. If it is a failure, the character gets tired and can’t move in the **next** time unit. Characters with high strength, particularly in the rare brave class, tend to use the straining mechanism a lot. 

If a boosted time unit covers a smaller time unit, such as when the party is ambushed en route (6 hours → round), a *new* straining roll is required for the new time unit, if the character wants to retain boosted movement.

[^1]: In some older modules, though, “turn” designates a time unit, as explained in the optional rules for dungeon and hex crawling in the next chapter (p. 69).
