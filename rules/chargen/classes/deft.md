Deft characters rely on superior technique and skill. They may for example be thieves, wandering monks, spies, marksmen, rangers or assassins. At level 1, the Deft must choose their vocation group (p. 42), but without marking it next to an attribute. When properly equipped, and when rolling for a task or an attack in line with the vocation, they always use a positive double roll. When they have combat advantage (p. 59), the Deft may swap this benefit for double damage, *if* their vocation is relevant. A trader might do this when defending her cargo, and an assassin when striking from the shadows.

As they advance, the Deft also get slots, each of which they permanently associate with *two* attunements. A deft attunement must be a teacher, an item, a vehicle, a pet or a place. For example, a ranger could have a well-trained dog or be attuned to her ancestral lands. A monk could have a famous master or be attuned to her bow. Using attunements requires active focus. F*or each slot, only one attunement is in the foreground* (i.e. active in the slot) while the other is inactive. Switching them takes a day spent in practice. Deciding which attunements to put into focus before a part of an adventure is a tactical element in deft preparations.

Active attunements can be invoked for a related action once per day to give the Deft **special rules for difficulty**: A hard task succeeds automatically, while a *nigh* impossible one requires a regular task roll (cf. p. 49). The ranger attuned to her ancestral lands could track on bare rock with a regular roll, while the monk attuned to her bow could hit a sentry at 200 feet automatically. Truly impossible feats remain impossible.

Lost attunements cannot be replaced but remain as keywords. They give a constant +1 to related tasks or attacks when active. The Deft have −2 av with non-attuned two-handed melee weapons, but get +1 to damage and df from an off-hand weapon if their vocation is combat oriented. A non-combat oriented vocation gives the Deft the option to save once per session, in order to turn a successful *task roll* into a critical success.

Deft expertise requires undisturbed movement and senses. If the character uses a shield or armor heavier than studded leather, she can neither use her slot abilities, nor switch positive double rolls for double damage.
