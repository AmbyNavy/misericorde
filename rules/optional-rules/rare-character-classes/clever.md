Clever characters think in different ways. They may not always have a high intelligence score, and they can be ordinary in many respects—in their training, in their physique and in their intuition. But in return, they are often curious, crafty and unafraid of failure. The Clever may for example be investigators, scientists, engineers, pioneers and explorers. 

Once per day, the Clever get a hefty +6 bonus for an unorthodox attempt to solve a non-combat related problem. Out of the box thinking can for example involve odd tools or materials, or apply a strange or at least original method. Further modifications can be applied, usually a negative one to account for the fact that a particular idea also a bit bizarre with some serious drawback. 

The clever treat all successful or critical rolls for clues, information gathering and detection as a *pair*, meaning that they get something extra out of it (p. 49). **This something is based on a question from the player**, but should be a) within bounds of the situation,[^1] b) proportionate to the quality of the roll and c) an *addition* not in itself surpassing the effect of the regular success or crit. For example, a clever character using intelligence to get a clue for a puzzle or to extract information from a book, simply gets a minor additional clue or piece of information, in the direction indicated by the player’s question. 

As they advance, the Clever also get slots for special *knacks*, rationalized (as they are used) in the most suitable manner for the vocation. They are chosen permanently from the following list:

1. The Clever can exploit combat situations. The base bonus for combat advantage is +3 instead of +2, and once per battle they may switch the d6 for the d10 as damage die.
2. When crafting, mending or putting something together, the Clever get +4, take half the time and can skip one part without loss of functionality, unless it is the most important part.
3. Targets of their special attacks get −3 for their saves.
4. The Clever can always figure out roughly where they are in a certain place. They don’t get lost.
5. The Clever get +2 to task rolls and saves in conviction attempts, in addition to any other modification based on what is said, offered, threatened with etc. This applies to trade as well.
6. The Clever are hard to confine or keep out, and get +4 to any task roll related to that.
7. The Clever are particularly good with substances—chemicals, herbs, poisons etc. They get +4 to identification and saves, as well as +1 in the character’s favor to any quantified effect.
8. The Clever have +4 to task rolls with or concerning machines.
9. The Clever get +4 to tracking and to cover their own tracks.

The Clever get an extra language from the start. At even levels, they can eschew the raise either for an additional language or for a ritual based on a slottable (p. 36) scroll, in a language the character knows. The Clever don’t slot it (like the Wise), but painstakingly learn it, permanently and without roll after a week’s study which also spends the scroll’s magick. Add 1 to the scroll HP cost and then double that to get the ritual cost. The casting time is either 10 minutes or double the time of the original scroll, whichever is longest. The Clever can only perform a certain ritual once per day. 

The Clever get +2 to SV vs. illusions and to any vocation related appraisal attempt. They can use any armor but get −2 AV with heavy weapons.

[^1]: Questions are very versatile and powerful tools, but the Referee isn’t obliged to answer a question that is evidently out of bounds. You cannot, for example, succesfully find out about a lost temple in a tome of history, and then ask “who is the killer in the murder mystery we are investigating?” The Referee can always say “I have nothing to give you in that direction. Please ask again.”
